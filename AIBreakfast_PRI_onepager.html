AI Breakfast Warsaw · 8 Sep 2026 · Andrzej Lipka (AL) n = 1 self-study · hypotheses for a physiotherapist, not a diagnosis

Four photos, ten injury predictions, fifteen years of MRIs to check them against.

I gave a multimodal LLM four posture photos of myself and one line of history: 46, climbing for 25 years, 20 years at a desk. It locked ten predicted injury sites, by side, before it saw any medical record. Then I scored it against my own imaging from 2012 to 2025. Later I did the same with walking video, using two models that had to agree.

Why I did it

PersonalPrevention over repair. Twenty-five years of climbing, running and biking left me with two knee surgeries, a shoulder repair and a stack of recurring niggles. I wanted to know which asymmetries are loading which structures, so I can work on them before the next injury.

CuriosityHow far has vision got? A year earlier the models could barely describe a posture photo. I wanted to see what today's models do on the two things physiotherapists actually look at: standing posture and gait.

BusinessPhysios lose money in two places: finding new patients and keeping them through a plan. A free movement check on a physio's link brings a lead with a report before the first visit. A repeat check at week six shows progress, which is what keeps people in the plan. The physio stays the one who examines and decides.

The input

The model saw face-blurred photos. This public copy blurs the whole body.

How it works

Posture, from four photos

  1. Four standard views on a phone.Front, both sides, back. Bare feet, plain wall, arms hanging free.
  2. Face blurred on the phone, before upload.MediaPipe face detection plus canvas blur in the browser. The original never leaves the phone. The server only ever sees blurred bytes.
  3. Model checks the photos are usable.Socks, patterned shorts, hands on clothing get flagged. The first set I shot was rejected for exactly that.
  4. Model reads the posture through the PRI lens.Postural Restoration Institute: a framework of typical left/right asymmetries. It called a textbook Left AIC + Right BC pattern.
  5. Predictions locked, then scored.Ten most likely injury sites, with side and mechanism, written down before any health data. Graded against MRI and ultrasound reports, misses included.

Gait, from six walking clips

  1. Six phone clips.Front, back and side, at normal and hurried pace. Tripod at hip height.
  2. Two models, two architectures.Gemini 3 Pro reads the raw video at 10 frames per second. Claude Opus reads 96 extracted stills. Neither is allowed to output numbers.
  3. One report, sorted by agreement.Findings both models made, findings only one made, and their disagreements, each cited to a timestamp or frame.

Side prediction held for the spine and legs and failed for the arms. Climbing loads the dominant hand harder than posture does, and the model had no handedness input.

Gait: where two models agreed

Both models, independently, on the same six clips:

  • Agreed anterior pelvic tilt with a deep lumbar curve, held through the whole stride.
  • Agreed right arm swings less than the left.
  • Agreed right foot turns out more than the left.
  • Agreed right shoulder sits lower, head drifts right.
  • Disagreed on the upper back: Gemini saw a left shoulder blade lifting off the ribcage, Claude saw a right-sided picture. That item went to the physio.

Both converged on the same PRI pattern the photos had produced a month earlier. Every finding is a candidate for a hands-on test, cited to a timestamp or a frame, with what the models could not see listed at the end.
